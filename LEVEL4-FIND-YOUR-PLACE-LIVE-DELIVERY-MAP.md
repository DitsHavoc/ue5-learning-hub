# Level 4 — Find Your Place
## Presentation + Hub live-delivery map

Use the presentation as the **story / explanation spine** and the Hub as the **active evidence layer**.

The repeated rhythm is:

**DELIVER → DISCUSS → HUB CHECKPOINT → SAVE → SCENARIO CHECK → CONTINUE**

## Chapter 1 — The Industry
**Presentation:** slides 5–39  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/industry`

**v3.49.2 Watch Along starter — Industry Reality Check**
- Use the first-instinct cards *before* fully revealing the answer in the presentation.
- The student's first answer is saved and then explained with a real case.
- Teacher class view shows an aggregate Class Pulse with no student names.
- Suggested early cards: Who Made It? → Bigger = Better? → Big Owner = Safer?
- Return to 38 Studios, Skull and Bones, RDR2 and GoldenEye as those anecdotes naturally fit the delivery.
- **Your Magnum Opus** is an optional two-stage crunch debate; it is not a chapter gate or graded quiz.

Pause for the Hub when the class has enough context to investigate rather than guess.

**Required checkpoint**
- Follow one real game's credits: developer, publisher, engine, external contributors and conclusion.
- Explore the UK Games Map and save one cluster plus studios found there.

**Return-to-class question**
> How useful is the phrase “the developer” when one released game may depend on many companies?

**Complete when:** checkpoint saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

**Session 1 hidden reading check**
- At the very end of the Industry page, students who actually read to the bottom receive the **Secret Studio Protocol**: quietly walk to the door, do one slow turn, return to their seat and continue as if nothing happened.
- If moving around is not practical, the accessible alternative is one silent thumbs-up to the teacher.
- No XP, no button and no completion gate. The physical response is the joke and the listening/reading check.

## Chapter 2 — The People Who Make Games
**Presentation:** slides 40–81  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/people`

Use the presentation role stories first. Then let students explore the Role Explorer rather than asking them to choose a career immediately.

**Required checkpoint**
- React to at least three roles: **Definitely / Maybe / Not for me**.
- Read the problem the role solves, collaborators, hard skills, soft skills and portfolio evidence before choosing.

**Return-to-class question**
> Which role surprised you most once you looked at the problems it actually solves?

**Complete when:** 3+ role reactions + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

## Chapter 3 — You as a Games Developer
**Presentation:** slides 82–99  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/you`

This is the self-discovery section. The work-style activity is deliberately **not a career diagnosis** and does not allocate a job from personality type.

**Required checkpoint**
- Complete the work-style reflection.
- Evidence at least one hard skill.
- Evidence at least one soft skill.
- Save peer feedback: one strength + one improvement.

**Return-to-class question**
> Which claimed strength can you actually prove, and which one currently depends mostly on confidence?

**Complete when:** all four evidence elements saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

## Chapter 4 — Careers Aren't Straight Lines
**Presentation:** slides 100–120  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/routes`

Use the interactive stories as predictions. Students commit to what they think happens **before** seeing the reveal.

**Required checkpoint**
- Predict at least one real career story.
- Save one personal lesson from the cases.

Stories include Narbacular Drop → Portal, Mel Cummings, Kristrun Fridriksdottir and The Forgotten City.

**Return-to-class question**
> Which apparently sideways move created useful experience rather than delaying the career?

**Complete when:** prediction + lesson saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

## Chapter 5 — Reality Check
**Presentation:** slides 121–136  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/reality`

Move from career descriptions to current employer evidence.

**Required checkpoint**
- Choose one specific working target role.
- Save three relevant vacancy snapshots.
- Create Green / Amber / Red evidence for repeated requirements.
- Identify at least one RED requirement and the biggest current gap.

**Return-to-class question**
> Does the role you liked still look right after reading what employers actually ask for?

**Complete when:** role + 3 vacancies + RED gap + biggest gap saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

---

## Chapter 6 — Prove It
**Presentation:** slides 137–157  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/prove`

The portfolio piece begins with an evidence gap, not with “what would be fun to make?”.

**Required checkpoint**
- Choose the targeted portfolio challenge.
- Name the employer requirement it will prove.
- State what will deliberately be cut so the piece stays finishable.

**Return-to-class question**
> If an employer saw only this one piece, what specific capability would it prove?

**Complete when:** challenge + purpose + scope cut saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.

---

## Chapter 7 — My Career Plan
**Presentation:** slides 158–182  
**Hub:** `#/industry-careers/plan`

This is where the Assignment 1 evidence becomes a defensible next direction rather than a vague ambition.

**Required checkpoint**
- Save a measurable 6-month goal.
- Save a measurable 12-month goal.
- Save a measurable 24-month goal.
- Review/copy the generated career summary as a starter for the assessed portfolio.

**Return-to-class question**
> What evidence should exist in six months that does not exist today?

**Complete when:** all three time horizons saved + 4/5 scenario questions correct.
